## What is supposed to transfer

The thing being tested is not a motion copied from a person. It is a procedure: the body's specification, a physical objective, and a support contract, searched in simulation, with no demonstration. A new task or a new body is supposed to go through that same procedure. Expert human movement can say what a task requires. It does not say how this robot's joints have to move.

Everything below is MuJoCo. None of it is a hardware result. A number at one timestep is not counted as valid. The two timesteps are 1 ms and 0.5 ms.

## What has transferred

**Throw, after the actuator-envelope audit.** The standing G1 throw that stays inside the declared motor speeds releases at 16.0 m/s, valid at both timesteps. An earlier 25 m/s figure drove the wrist past its rated speed and is withdrawn. Do not use it.

**Pick and place, frozen generator, G1, eight held-out setups.** The generator was tagged and then run on object positions, masses, sizes, and table heights it was not tuned on. No task-specific code for those eight. The grasp is an abstract weld, and the run sees simulator state. On the freeze tagged `freeze-gen-v1`, five of the eight land inside 6 cm and seven of eight land inside 7.2 cm. The 6 cm line sits inside a couple of centimetres of run-to-run scatter, so the two near-misses (a 0.8 kg cube at 6.5 cm, a light small cube at 6.1 cm) are misses by the rule and not a different kind of failure. This harness searched at 1 ms. It did not replay the same solution at 0.5 ms, so this row is not dual-timestep.

**The same entry point on the H1.** One scene: pick a cube off a 0.95 m table and place it 6.0 cm from the target, every step valid. That is one scene, not a held-out set, and the grasp geometry is declared per robot.

**Pinch, both timesteps, shapes the controller had not been fit to.** A clearance-based finger opening, the same rule for every object, passes 0.78 of sixty sealed conditions (95% interval 0.66–0.87) and 0.80 of a second sealed sixty (0.68–0.88). The plate comes along. The mug stays at zero: a pinch cannot take a handle. A later hook lifts the mug it was built on (10 of 10, including two held-out shoves) and does not lift the other loops it agrees to try (0 of 3 at both timesteps). That hook is fitted to the mug. A sealed loop suite was not opened for it.

**A push.** Palms on a rigid wall, no weld, holds 20 N at both timesteps. With the contact law off, the same setup puts 2–3 N on the wall. It fails at 40 N. A spec-only prediction of 77 N was two to four times high.

## What has not

The pick-place case that fails on every freeze is the cube set 0.18 m in front of the body. The grasp and the lift succeed. The carry does not: a non-foot part hits the floor, a foot unloads, and the cube is not placed. Same mass and same table as a case 7 cm farther out that does place.

Tilted surfaces and a longer tool were never run. There is no held-out H1 manipulation set. Household tasks, cloth, and laundry are not results.

The box-jump count lives in [Where the box jumps fail](/posts/robotic-movement/box-jump-failures/) and [The line over the landing](/posts/robotic-movement/the-line/). It is not re-measured here.

## 2026-10-30 — the close cube, at a smaller budget

The published 5 of 8 was searched at 1 ms only. I re-ran the cube at 0.18 m and the one at 0.25 m with the controller unchanged, at 20 search iterations and 12 adaptation iterations instead of the original 80 and 150. This is not a new score out of eight.

At 1 ms both carries stayed valid. Neither cube fell. The farther one was placed at 6.01 cm, a tenth of a millimetre outside the 6 cm rule. The closer one was placed at 6.85 cm. Replaying those same solutions at 0.5 ms, with no further search, kept the farther cube valid (5.35 cm). The closer cube's lift ended unbalanced, and its carry lost a foot and never reached.

Across every rollout in that pair, no knee or hand touched the floor. The first bad event was always one foot going to zero load while the other foot still held the weight. On the 1 ms runs that lasted less than the quarter-second the stance rule allows, so the step still passed. The historical fall, the one that left the cube 94 cm away with a non-foot part on the floor, did not show up at this shorter search. I am not calling that fall explained.

The gap this run actually found: a solution accepted at 1 ms does not have to survive 0.5 ms, and the pick-and-place search never asks it to. The throw and the jump already score the worse of the two timesteps.

## 2026-10-30 — searching at both timesteps

Same budget, on a cube that is not one of the eight, at (0.16 m, −0.30 m). One seed.

| search | 1 ms | 0.5 ms |
|---|---|---|
| 1 ms only | steps valid, placed 8.8 cm | carry does not reach, placed 9.2 cm |
| worse of the two timesteps | steps valid, placed 6.4 cm | steps valid, placed 6.9 cm |

Scoring both timesteps removed the 0.5 ms failure and cut the miss. It did not get inside 6 cm. The cube is on the table either way. The vertical 3 cm is the target point sitting above where a 6 cm cube rests, and the rule allows that. The eight published setups were not re-run, so the 5 of 8 number is unchanged. A second seed of that search is below.

## 2026-10-30 — the second seed

Same cube, same budget, both-timesteps search, seed 2. At 1 ms every step was valid and the cube was placed at 7.2 cm. At 0.5 ms the carry did not reach, and the cube was at 7.5 cm. The cube was on the table both times.

Seed 1 had placed it at 6.4 cm and 6.9 cm with every step valid. Seed 2 is worse, and it loses the 0.5 ms carry that seed 1 kept. Two seeds are not a pass.

The miss is not a cloud around the target. On both seeds and both timesteps the cube's x is within 2 cm of the target, and its y is 6 to 7 cm too far toward the side it was picked up from. The 6.4 cm number was the better of the two seeds. The published five of eight was not re-run.

## 2026-10-30 — the miss is the palm

The second seed was run again and landed on the same place, 7.2 cm. At the moment of release the palm itself is 6.7 cm too far toward the pickup side. The weld puts the cube another 0.9 cm that way, and it is mostly a vertical offset: the cube sits 5.7 cm above the palm. Letting the cube settle for half a second with the robot frozen moves it 0.6 cm back toward the target. The palm is the miss. Its height matches the target, and its x is within 1.3 cm.

That carry never got a second search. The palm's closest approach was 5.5 cm, which the reach rule counts as arrived, so adaptation did not run. The cube was 8.2 cm away horizontally. The rule grades a palm fly-by. The published number grades the cube after it is let go.

A re-run of the first seed did not repeat its 6.4 cm place. It placed at 7.7 cm. The split was the same: the palm was 6.6 cm short in that direction. The published five of eight was not re-run.

## 2026-10-30 — scoring the cube, the hand still leaves

The carry was then scored on the cube's horizontal distance instead of the palm. Same budget, both seeds. The carry did adapt, which it had not done when the palm's fly-by was enough. It still did not place inside 6 cm.

On one seed the cube's closest approach was 4.5 cm and the settled cube was 8.4 cm away. On the other the closest approach was 5.5 cm and the cube settled at 7.0 cm. The hand passes through the target and leaves. The rule counts the pass. The published five of eight was not re-run.

## 2026-10-30 — requiring the cube at release

The carry was then required to have the cube inside 6 cm at the moment of release, not merely to have passed through that ball. Twelve adaptation steps did not do it. The search fell through to a waypoint that does not release the cube. Against the waypoint the step looks valid. Against the place target the cube settles 15 cm and 21 cm away, on the table. The published five of eight was not re-run.

## 2026-10-30 — forty steps, on this one cube

Forty adaptation steps, with the cube required to be inside 6 cm at release, placed this cube on both seeds. Seed 1 landed at 3.1 cm at 1 ms and 3.4 cm at 0.5 ms. Seed 2 landed at 1.1 cm and 2.0 cm. Every step was valid, and the cube was on the table. The carry that ran was the real one, not the waypoint.

This cube is not one of the eight published setups, and those were not re-run. Five of eight is unchanged. One cube at one position is not a transfer result. A closer cube, written down before this one passed, is the next measurement.

## 2026-10-30 — an earlier landing change, on one stored failure

The counted box-jump controller still lands the undisturbed jump at both timesteps. The hip-to-head line ends about 4 degrees forward. On one stored trial, friction at 0.8 times stock, the box 1 cm closer, and a small controller nudge, it falls at both timesteps. At touchdown the line is 0.90 rad forward at 1 ms and 0.94 rad at 0.5 ms. The fall ends at 1.12 rad. Liftoff pitch is 0.35 rad, against 0.33 rad on the jump that lands.

Two earlier changes were tried, each on its own. Less forward pitch in the dip, by 0.10 rad. A slower push, by 0.03 s. Each one made the undisturbed jump fall as well, and the stored trial still fell. Neither was kept. The hundred trials were not re-run. Forty-nine of one hundred is not a certified landing.

## 2026-10-30 — a mass shift and a placement gain, on the same fall

The counted jump was run again. It still lands. The same stored trial still falls, with the line at 0.90 rad at touchdown.

Moving the dip's mass 3 cm back was achieved. The undisturbed jump then fell the other way, and the line ended 1.24 rad with the head behind the hips. The stored trial also fell, and at 1 ms its feet were not on the box. Neither was kept.

Raising the in-flight foot placement gain by 0.20 left the undisturbed jump landing. The stored trial still fell, and at 1 ms the touchdown line was the same 0.90 rad. The gain changed. The fall did not. The hundred trials were not re-run.
