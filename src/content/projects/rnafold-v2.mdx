## The problem

The [Stanford RNA 3D Folding](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/stanford-rna-3d-folding)
competition asks for the 3D coordinates of every nucleotide in an RNA molecule, given only its
sequence. Entries submit five structures per target and are scored by TM-score against the
experimentally determined structure. TM-score runs from 0 to 1: it rewards residues that land close
to their true position after the two structures are superimposed, with a distance scale that grows
with length, so a long RNA is not punished for small local errors.

It is hard for three reasons. There are far fewer solved RNA structures than protein structures
(the competition training set has 5,716 sequences, and the PDB-derived template bank I used has
9,564 entries). RNA folds are dominated by base pairing and long-range tertiary contacts that a
sequence alone does not pin down. And the test targets are real recent depositions: the 28 test
sequences I worked with range from 19 nt to a 4,640-nt complex.

## Local results

The repo scores RNAPro, an open diffusion-based structure model, on the competition test
sequences whose structures are now in the PDB. The protocol, from `pipeline5.ipynb`:

- **Targets:** 24 of the 28 test sequences. Two (4,640 and 1,460 nt) are over RNAPro's 500-nt cap,
  and two more (9LEC, 9LEL) ran out of memory on the 8 GB card.
- **Prediction:** RNAPro with MSAs and RibonanzaNet2 features, no templates, 2 recycles,
  8 diffusion steps. The score uses **one sample per target** (the first sample of the first seed),
  not the competition's best of five.
- **Reference:** the deposited mmCIF from RCSB, reduced to one point per nucleotide (the centroid of
  P, C4′ and C1′).
- **Residue pairing:** the predicted chain is aligned to the reference sequence (local alignment),
  and only aligned residues are compared.
- **Score:** a TM-score computed in Python after a least-squares (Kabsch) superposition, normalised
  by the number of paired residues. It approximates TM-align rather than reproducing the official
  scorer: TM-align searches for the superposition that maximises TM-score, which can only raise the
  number, and the official score normalises by the full reference length, which can lower it.

| RNAPro, local evaluation (n = 24 targets) | TM-score |
|---|---|
| Mean | **0.072** |
| Median | 0.043 |
| Best target (9G4J, 241 paired residues) | 0.238 |
| Mean residues paired per target | 101 |

No baseline was scored in the repo, so the only in-repo calibration is RNAPro's own confidence: its
ranking scores averaged 0.030 across its outputs, so the model was not confident in these structures
either. The notebook printed per-target rows for only the first 10 targets; the summary statistics
above cover all 24.

Two other local numbers in the repo are not usable. The first scoring harness
(`evaluation.ipynb`) ran TM-align on all 130 sample files and got **no** scores: TM-align could
not parse any of the 125 converted prediction files ("Cannot parse file … Chain number 0"), and the
reference for the remaining target (9I9W) failed conversion because its chain ID is too long for PDB
format. The result CSVs record exactly that: 125 rows with empty TM-score columns, 5 failures, and an
empty best-per-target file. The second, in `pipeline3.ipynb`, compared backbone atoms by position,
which lined a short prediction up against the first atoms of a much larger reference. Its
best-of-five mean of 0.093 is an artefact of that pairing, so the repo has no valid best-of-five
versus single-sample comparison.

## Where the signal came from

The only held-out signal in the repo is RNAPro's, and it is small. Across the 10 targets with
printed rows, RNAPro did best on the longer, structured ones: 9G4J (0.238), 9E74 (0.158), 9E9Q
(0.147) and 9E75 (0.135). Short targets such as 9G4P (33 paired residues, 0.010) and 8ZNQ (30, 0.030)
score near zero. Part of that is the metric: the TM-score distance scale shrinks for short chains, so
a few ångströms of error costs a 30-nt RNA much more than a 250-nt one. Over those 10 targets, paired
length and score have a rank correlation of 0.76.

I can't attribute RNAPro's score to its inputs. The run used MSAs and RibonanzaNet2 together and
never turned either off, so there is no ablation of MSA, language-model features or templates in
the repo.

The evaluation work moved the numbers more than any modelling change did. Pairing residues by
sequence instead of by position moved 9G4J from 0.049 to 0.238 and the 24-target mean from 0.059 to
0.072.

## Why this architecture

The repo has two tracks that share one evaluation stack.

**RNAPro first** (`pipeline1.ipynb`, `pipeline2.ipynb`). Running a 488-million-parameter
diffusion model on an 8 GB card meant rebuilding its chemical-component cache, enabling expandable
CUDA memory segments, capping length at 500 nt and keeping diffusion steps and recycles low. That
produced 5 samples for each of 24 targets, with low scores and low model confidence.

**Measuring it properly** (`evaluation.ipynb`, `pipeline3.ipynb`, `pipeline4.ipynb`). TM-align
could not read the converted files, so I moved scoring into Python. The first version compared atoms
by position; `pipeline4.ipynb` replaced that with one pseudo-atom per nucleotide, and
`pipeline5.ipynb` finally paired residues by sequence alignment. `pipeline4.ipynb` also built the
template database (9,564 PDB RNA structures, parsed in about an hour) and a GPU FAISS index.

**A template-based folder** (`pipeline5.ipynb`). With the diffusion model capped at 500 nt and
run with few steps, I built a folder with no neural network: retrieve similar structures from the
PDB bank, align the query to each, copy coordinates across, fuse the candidates into a weighted
consensus, fill gaps, then refine. Its stated goal was a local TM-score of 0.5. Every stage writes an
inspectable artifact and is benchmarked on its own, so a stage that hurts can be switched off. On
that benchmark, superposing candidates before fusing and reweighting templates by agreement
(recycling) raised the score, distogram reconstruction and the contact-graph constraint lowered it,
and fragment and motif repair changed nothing.

In the same sequence-matched evaluation (`pipeline5.ipynb`, the cell that scores both models on the
same 24 targets) the template folder reaches a mean TM-score of **0.583** (median 0.558, best 0.975),
against 0.072 for RNAPro. I do not count that as a prediction score. That benchmark is also where the
design went wrong: the template bank contained each target's own deposited structure (top-1 retrieval returned
a sequence-identical entry for every target, and in 9 of the 10 rows the notebook printed it was the target's own PDB entry), and the evaluation
fed the reference sequence in as the query. The folder was rebuilding the answer from a copy of the
answer, so its scores measure how faithfully coordinates are transferred, not how well a structure
is predicted, and the stage-by-stage directions above may not hold on unseen RNAs. A real test has to
remove each target and its close homologs from the bank first; that run is not in the repo.

## Limits

The one usable number covers 24 targets, one sample each, scored with an approximate TM-score rather
than the competition's US-align best-of-five. The predicted structures are not in the repo, so these
scores can't be recomputed from coordinates, only traced to the notebook cells that printed them.
Four test targets were never predicted. The template folder has never been run with the targets held
out, so whether it beats RNAPro is open.
