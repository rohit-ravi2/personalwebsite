I’m an undergraduate researcher at NYU’s Center for Data Science, part of the Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences. My interests bring together machine learning, computational neuroscience, and the analysis of biological data.

My work includes computational studies of *C. elegans* connectivity and gene expression, alongside machine-learning projects in neural decoding and structured prediction. Across these projects, I develop data-processing pipelines, train and adapt models, and investigate how evaluation choices affect the conclusions we can draw.

## Selected work

The broader portfolio includes:

- [MEG speech decoding in LibriBrain](/projects/libribrain-2026): model development and an investigation of timing information in the evaluation.
- [Handwriting decoding from wrist EMG](/projects/handwriting-emg): reproducing a released model and examining adaptation to individual writers.
- [C. elegans connectivity and gene expression](/projects/c-elegans-multimodal): a learning pipeline for studying the relationship between network structure and biological measurements.

Each project page describes its own scope and status. [About me](/about) explains the personal motivation behind these interests.
