## The puzzle

A pyramidal neuron in visual cortex receives thousands of synapses spread along its dendrites.
Spine-imaging studies (Wilson et al. 2016; Iacaruso et al. 2017) found that neighbouring spines
tend to have more similar tuning than distant ones. The appealing reading is that the brain places
inputs carrying related information next to each other, so the dendrite can combine them locally.

Imaging cannot test that reading. It sees the spine but not the presynaptic cell, so it cannot tell
input-specific placement apart from something more mundane: neurons whose cell bodies sit near each
other send axons through the same neuropil and have overlapping receptive fields, so their synapses
would land near each other on a dendrite even if nothing sorted them by function.

The MICrONS volume of mouse visual cortex can separate the two. It has the reconstructed dendrites,
the synapse positions on them, the presynaptic neurons and their soma positions, and functional
profiles for 13,805 co-registered presynaptic neurons from a model fit to their recorded activity.
The question I asked: **do functionally similar inputs land near each other along the dendrite,
beyond what presynaptic geometry predicts?**

Code and results: [github.com/rohit-ravi2/microns-dendritic](https://github.com/rohit-ravi2/microns-dendritic)

## What survived

The plot sets the three quantities side by side for each test: the naive excess produced by labels
that contain only soma position (open circles), the naive excess with the real functional labels
(red), and the geometry-adjusted estimate with its 95% null interval (blue). The real raw effect is
no larger than geometry alone makes it, and the adjusted estimate sits on zero everywhere, including
in the second animal.

## Findings and limits

The finding I am most confident in is about method. On MICrONS, a within-cell shuffle of presynaptic
identities, the obvious way to test dendritic clustering, reports "clustering" at p = 0.001 when
the labels are nothing but presynaptic soma position (d = 0.095 on pyramidal dendrites, 0.139 on
interneurons). The same happens in V1DD (d = 0.201, p = 0.002). Any shuffle-based analysis of
input clustering in these volumes will find geometry and can call it function.

The substantive result is a bounded null. Near-pair similarity is genuinely elevated on the
dendrite (d = 0.056 for receptive-field location on pyramidal cells, 0.032 for the tuning feature on
interneurons), but it is the size geometry produces, and once presynaptic soma distance, lateral
separation and axon co-travel are regressed out, nothing is left that the tests detect: null in all
4 confirmatory tests, in 87 robustness runs (smallest Holm p = 0.279), on a held-out
tier of 12,522 presynaptic neurons, in a path-vs-Euclidean test that does not use the geometry model,
on spine-only synapses, and in the Sarup & Boahen axon groups. In the broad tier, the best-powered
version, the upper 95% bounds on placement beyond geometry are d ≤ 0.019–0.030 for receptive-field
and tuning-feature similarity, and d ≤ 0.019 for receptive-field location on pyramidal dendrites. The adjusted test had at least 0.88 power at d = 0.10 on blind data.

The claim is "no placement beyond presynaptic soma geometry", not "no clustering". Placement that
follows retinotopic axon routing counts as geometry here by construction. And the limits are real:

- **One main animal, model-derived labels.** The MICrONS profiles come from a digital-twin model of
  the recorded activity, and co-registration covers a small fraction of each cell's inputs. The
  V1DD replication uses measured activity but has only 958 near pairs (smallest detectable effect
  d ≈ 0.1–0.2), and uses straight-line rather than along-dendrite distance.
- **The apical tuft is untested in practice.** It has 185 near pairs in the broad tier, giving
  bounds of 0.24–0.57. That is the compartment tuft-imaging studies point to, so an effect could
  still be there.
- **A small positive residual.** The pyramidal receptive-field estimate is slightly positive in the
  strict tier (+0.027), the held-out tier (+0.035, z = 1.73) and V1DD natural movies (+0.046), and
  negative in the broad tier (−0.013). None is significant. A pooled test was not pre-registered,
  so I treat this as a bound, not an effect.
- **The planted-geometry check only tests geometry of the form I modelled.** The replications that
  do not use the geometry model (held-out labels, path vs Euclidean, spine-only, a second animal)
  are what cover the rest, and they agree.

Open, each with its next step: a path-distance V1DD run (needs authenticated skeleton access), V1DD
labels built from measured tuning rather than correlations, a tuft test with roughly ten times the
pairs, and ordered axon groups of three or more once a full group list is available.

<p style={{fontSize: "0.8rem", opacity: 0.7, marginTop: "1.5rem"}}>
Scope: the data and functional co-registration are the MICrONS Consortium's (Ding et al., Nature 2025,
which tests function against connectivity at the level of neuron pairs, not synapse position on the
dendrite). The clustering being tested comes from spine-imaging studies (Wilson et al. 2016; Iacaruso
et al. 2017), which cannot see presynaptic cells. Sarup and Boahen (bioRxiv 2026) found anatomical
axon groups that co-cluster on branches, without functional data; this project gives them a
functional test. As of October 2026 I found no prior test that combines measured presynaptic
function, synapse position along the dendrite, and a presynaptic-geometry control. The contribution
is the geometry-confound result, the bounded null with held-out and second-animal replications, and
the pre-registered record of which of my predictions failed.
</p>
