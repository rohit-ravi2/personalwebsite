## What is supposed to transfer

The thing being tested is not a motion copied from a person. It is a procedure: the body's specification, a physical objective, and a support contract, searched in simulation, with no demonstration. A new task or a new body is supposed to go through that same procedure. Expert human movement can say what a task requires. It does not say how this robot's joints have to move.

Everything below is MuJoCo. None of it is a hardware result. A number at one timestep is not counted as valid. The two timesteps are 1 ms and 0.5 ms.

## What has transferred

**Throw, after the actuator-envelope audit.** The standing G1 throw that stays inside the declared motor speeds releases at 16.0 m/s, valid at both timesteps. An earlier 25 m/s figure drove the wrist past its rated speed and is withdrawn. Do not use it.

**Pick and place, frozen generator, G1, eight held-out setups.** The generator was tagged and then run on object positions, masses, sizes, and table heights it was not tuned on. No task-specific code for those eight. The grasp is an abstract weld, and the run sees simulator state. On the freeze tagged `freeze-gen-v1`, five of the eight land inside 6 cm and seven of eight land inside 7.2 cm. The 6 cm line sits inside a couple of centimetres of run-to-run scatter, so the two near-misses (a 0.8 kg cube at 6.5 cm, a light small cube at 6.1 cm) are misses by the rule and not a different kind of failure. This harness searched at 1 ms. It did not replay the same solution at 0.5 ms, so this row is not dual-timestep.

**The same entry point on the H1.** One scene: pick a cube off a 0.95 m table and place it 6.0 cm from the target, every step valid. That is one scene, not a held-out set, and the grasp geometry is declared per robot.

**Pinch, both timesteps, shapes the controller had not been fit to.** A clearance-based finger opening, the same rule for every object, passes 0.78 of sixty sealed conditions (95% interval 0.66–0.87) and 0.80 of a second sealed sixty (0.68–0.88). The plate comes along. The mug stays at zero: a pinch cannot take a handle. A later hook lifts the mug it was built on (10 of 10, including two held-out shoves) and does not lift the other loops it agrees to try (0 of 3 at both timesteps). That hook is fitted to the mug. A sealed loop suite was not opened for it.

**A push.** Palms on a rigid wall, no weld, holds 20 N at both timesteps. With the contact law off, the same setup puts 2–3 N on the wall. It fails at 40 N. A spec-only prediction of 77 N was two to four times high.

## What has not

The pick-place case that fails on every freeze is the cube set 0.18 m in front of the body. The grasp and the lift succeed. The carry does not: a non-foot part hits the floor, a foot unloads, and the cube is not placed. Same mass and same table as a case 7 cm farther out that does place.

Tilted surfaces and a longer tool were never run. There is no held-out H1 manipulation set. Household tasks, cloth, and laundry are not results.

The box-jump count lives in [Where the box jumps fail](/posts/robotic-movement/box-jump-failures/) and [The line over the landing](/posts/robotic-movement/the-line/). It is not re-measured here.

The gap this run actually found: a solution accepted at 1 ms does not have to survive 0.5 ms, and the pick-and-place search never asks it to. The throw and the jump already score the worse of the two timesteps.
